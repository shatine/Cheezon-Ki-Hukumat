Can you really live in a bus?

Kerala's KSRTC retires buses after 15 years and auctions them — some go for as little as ₹2 lakh. A roof, walls, windows, room for a bed, for less than a few years of rent. So why did people who turned an old bus into a home give up within a month?

First, heat: a metal body pulls the sun straight inside, and even full insulation leaves a path — the steel frame itself, a thermal bridge. The same metal turns cold at night, and the moisture from breathing, cooking and washing condenses inside the walls, where mould grows.
Then water and waste: the fresh tank empties and the waste tank fills every single day.
And finally land and law: the hose and cable need somewhere to plug in — and India's Supreme Court has ruled that no vehicle can be altered to change its original manufacturer specification.

We follow a couple who turned a Force Traveller into a camper, the Kerala YouTubers whose camper "Napoleon" was seized, and Kerala's caravan parks — to find out what ₹2 lakh actually buys.
