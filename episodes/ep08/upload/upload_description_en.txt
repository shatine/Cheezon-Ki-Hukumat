What happens to this bridge in an earthquake?

The Chenab Rail Bridge in Jammu and Kashmir carries trains 359 m above the riverbed — 35 m taller than the Eiffel Tower, about five Qutub Minars stacked on top of each other — and there isn't a single pillar standing in the river. Opened in June 2025, it is the highest railway bridge in the world. So how do you build a bridge where not even one pillar can stand?

First, the gorge: a deep V with slopes of 43° to 77°, cut into the young, still-rising Himalaya, where the rock is cracked and breaks easily. A tall pillar on such a slope wants to slide into the valley — so the engineers chose one 469 m steel arch that pushes its weight into both mountains.
Then the mountains had to be fixed before the bridge: cement grouting into the cracks, steel anchors pulled tight with jacks, and sprayed concrete over the slopes.
Then the arch was built in mid-air: a 915 m cable crane between two towers, half-arches held back by cables tied into the rock, and on 5 April 2021 the two halves met over the river.
And finally the earthquake, wind and sun: a deck resting on bearings with expansion joints, a design that keeps standing even if one pier is lost, wind tests up to 266 km/h, 120 sensors watching 24 hours a day, and rails that are left free to slide.
