## ① 213만: How India Built the World's Highest Railway Bridge (English, 11 min, 3D animation)
**흐름:**
1. A magnitude 8.0 earthquake hits — but the rail deck doesn't move.
2. Spherical bearings and expansion joints: the deck "floats".
3. Foundations: the mountain is cut back (slopes 43–77°, weak dolomite rock). Pre-splitting blasts.
4. Slope stabilisation in three steps:
   - Grouting: cement fills the cracks.
   - Anchor blocks: tensioned steel tendons press the block into the slope.
   - Shotcrete.
5. Piers → a 915 m cable crane (35 t) → arch segments 10–12 m long, 600,000 nuts and bolts. Stay cables hold each half-arch, balanced by back-stays.
6. Arch closure: hydraulic jacks set the gap and height, then the crown is fitted. Once joined, the halves support each other.
7. Deck: expansion joints split it into sections.
8. Why an arch rather than piers: piers would be too tall and would block the river.
9. Parabolic vs circular arch experiment. A hanging chain, flipped over, becomes a pure-compression arch.
10. Ballastless track, 100 km/h. Redundancy: if one pier is damaged, trains still cross at 30 km/h.

**장점:**
- The hook is a disaster that turns out unexpectedly fine.
- Every principle has a small experiment or an animation (tendon tension → slope pushed inward; the flipped chain).
- **This is the core reference for our 3D principle videos.**

## ② 158만: The B1M, India Built the World's Most Remote Bridge (English, 14 min, live footage)
**흐름:**
1. "Somewhere no human was meant to go": 50°C to below freezing, cliffs.
2. Access only on foot or by boat; 30,000 t of steel to move.
3. Wind up to 165 mph, plus earthquakes.
4. The biggest challenge is access: donkeys and a boat (engineer interview).
5. Why it was built: NH44 is regularly cut by snow. All-weather rail line of 169 miles, 943 bridges, 36 tunnels.
6. On-site fabrication workshops, 26 km of new roads, steel delivered as flat plate.
7. Canyon = Venturi funnel that speeds up the wind.
8. Young, fractured Himalayan rock: a tower fixed to one side of a V-shaped canyon tends to slide down.
   - **So an arch: the two halves push against each other and send the load into the mountain.**
9. Wind tunnel (1:50 model) plus local airflow models (+20% acceleration).
10. Cable crane; half-arches held back by cables anchored in the rock.
11. Deck launched in from each side.
12. Earthquakes: on a long span the relative shaking is small; the approaches are affected more.
13. Sun heats one side of the arch → it bends → the rails are pre-tensioned and free in their fixings.
14. 20 years.

**장점:**
- The environment comes first, building the "should have been impossible" feeling.
- It answers "why this way" with the arch in a V-shaped canyon.
- Thermal bending and the free-floating rails are a point the other two videos don't cover.

## ③ 120만: Animation के जरिए समझिए Chenab Rail Bridge (Hindi, 17 min, news + animation)
**흐름:**
1. A poetic opening: the Chenab as a river of lovers, and Partition-era poetry.
2. National pride.
3. Numbers in Indian comparisons: 35 m taller than the Eiffel Tower, five Qutub Minars, a 100-storey building. 1,315 m long, arch 467 m (550 m along the curve), arch weighs 10,619 t.
4. Challenges: seismic Zone V, no roads. 43–77° slopes = "like gluing a bridge to a wall".
5. History:
   - 1898: Maharaja Pratap Singh proposes the line; the British reject it over cost.
   - 2003: approved. 2004: work starts.
   - No access road until 2006. Then 5 km + 3 km of roads. 2008: contract awarded.
6. Young mountains: controlled blasting, IISc slope stabilisation (rock bolts, shotcrete, cable anchors).
7. Cable crane 915 m long, 127 m high, 35 t. 600,000 bolts.
8. Pier 137 m (about two Qutub Minars); foundation 50×36 m (half a football field).
9. Phased-array ultrasonic weld testing. Welding laid end to end = 584 km = Jammu to Delhi.
10. 70% local workforce; work continued at −20°C.
11. 5 April 2021, crown segment: wind 150 km/h, hydraulic jacks.
12. 3,200 people; arch finished 2022. Deck and track in the following 2 years.
13. Expansion joints.
14. Wind governs the design: 8 boxes filled with concrete for weight. Wind tunnel at Force Technology, Copenhagen.
15. Trains 100 km/h; wind 266 km/h; earthquake M8 (a claim).
16. NH44 cut by snow: Jammu → Kashmir by road was 350 km, 8–10 hours. Kashmir to Kanyakumari.

**장점:**
- **Comparisons Indian viewers know instantly** (Qutub Minar, Jammu–Delhi distance, half a football field).
- The history hook (a 120-year-old dream) and the local workers.

**주의:**
- Steel weight differs between sources: video ③ says 35,000 t, The B1M says 30,000 t, Wikipedia says 25,000 t of steel plus 4,000 t of reinforcing bar.
- The video also says "300 metric tonnes", likely a misspoken figure.
- The M8 earthquake figure is a government claim.

  4. But the mountain is crumbling.
  5. So the mountain was strengthened.
  6. Even standing, wind, heat and earthquakes remain.
  7. So the deck floats.

  - Canyon wind (Venturi).
  - Sun heating one side → the arch bends → the rails move freely.
