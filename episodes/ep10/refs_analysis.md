## ① BBC Earth Science, Fatberg (English, 4:23, presenter in the sewer)
**흐름:**
1. A covered river turned into a Victorian sewer (318 million bricks).
2. The worker's job, and the structure of side sewers → trunk sewer.
3. Bottleneck: fat collects where the flow narrows.
4. The fatberg: solidified fat plus other waste. Size, smell, it has to be broken up by hand.
5. End: "what starts as a bit of grease in your kitchen becomes a monster".

**장점:** the viewer is taken to the place; one physical reaction does the explaining. **The idea of the bottleneck** (where it builds up) can be shown in 3D.

## ② TED-Ed, Biodiesel: The afterlife of oil (English, 4:14, animation)
**흐름:**
1. Used oil → down the drain → blocks the drain and the sewer, floods.
2. Diesel engines run trucks, buses, ships, trains, generators.
3. Fossil diesel vs biodiesel (from plants and animal fat).
4. At home: cool it, filter it, put it in a container.
5. Cities collect it from restaurants → processing plant.
6. Principle: a triglyceride (glycerol + 3 fatty acid chains) + methanol → esters + glycerol. The esters look like diesel molecules.
7. Scale: one day of a big city's grease could power hundreds of flights.
8. End: the waste becomes something good.

**장점:** the whole of our chain (blockage → collection → conversion → engine) in 4 minutes. **Showing the molecule split into three as a 3D principle video** is the key reference. The comparison "looks like diesel if you squint" is visual.

## ③ MR. INDIAN HACKER, We Make Diesel At Home (Hindi, 13:38, experiment)
**흐름:**
1. Indian snacks → where does the oil go → what if it ran a vehicle.
2. Getting used oil from a snack shop.
3. Heat to 80°C, then cool to 60°C.
4. Methanol + potassium hydroxide (danger warning).
5. Mix, stir for 20 minutes → leave for days → separation (biodiesel on top, glycerol below; "it can make soap").
6. Wash with water 4–5 times.
7. Fire test: biodiesel vs diesel.
8. Engine test: B30 (30% bio) then B100. Warning that modern BS engines are designed for regular diesel.

**장점:** the steps are concrete and visible (the layers separating, the colour changing). **Blend percentages** (B30, B100) and the engine starting give a payoff. Our video uses its order of steps, but at **industrial scale** (collection vehicle → plant → truck), not at home.
